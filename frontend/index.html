SHAP & Gradient Saliency Methodology

The model interpretability engine computes input embedding gradients with respect to the output logit for the predicted class:

Attribution(w_i) = || ∇_Embed(w_i) ⊙ Embed(w_i) ||_2

This highlights which subwords contributed most heavily to the classification verdict, distinguishing sensational rhetoric from factual news phrasing.

Architecture & Pipeline

How the Hybrid Model Works

A deep dive into why combining multilingual transformer encoders with recurrent sequence layers and masked mean pooling enables robust cross-lingual detection.

Step 01

Multilingual Tokenization

SentencePiece tokenization with a 250,000 subword vocabulary enables seamless representation of Latin, Cyrillic, Devanagari, Arabic, and Tamil scripts without out-of-vocabulary loss.

Step 02

XLM-RoBERTa Transformer

12 transformer encoder layers map sentences into a shared cross-lingual latent space where semantically equivalent concepts in French, Hindi, and English share aligned vectors.

Step 03

Bidirectional LSTM

A 256-dimensional BiLSTM processes sequence hidden states in both forward and backward directions, capturing subtle rhetorical and deceptive flow across long articles.

Step 04

Masked Mean Pooling

Crucial innovation: standard pooling dilutes embeddings with padding tokens. Our masked mean pooling zeros out padding tokens using the attention mask, maintaining zero-shot transfer.

Step 05

Calibrated Classification Head

Dropout regularization followed by linear projection to 2 output classes yields well-calibrated probabilities for real and fake news determination.

Project Background

About & Research Foundation

This system is built upon state-of-the-art academic datasets and research in cross-lingual natural language processing.

The PolyglotFakeFacts Dataset

Published in February 2026, PolyglotFakeFacts comprises 10,206 full-length verified articles across 18 languages: Arabic, Armenian, Azerbaijani, Bulgarian, Czech, English, Finnish, French, Georgian, German, Hungarian, Italian, Lithuanian, Romanian, Russian, Slovak, Spanish, and Swedish.

Disinformation items are collected from the European Union's EUvsDisinfo monitoring desk, while authentic counterparts originate from trusted national publications.
